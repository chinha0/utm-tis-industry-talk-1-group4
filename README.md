## 🧩 Core Takeaways from the Talk

### 1. TIS in Practice (People, Hardware, Software, Data, Process)
Using MyREDS as a real-world community platform:
- People: Residents and guards → *Training and support*
- Hardware: Phones and gate devices → *Installation and upkeep*
- Software: Apps and dashboard → *Reliability and updates*
- Data: Access logs and reports → *Useful records and privacy*
- Process: Register, verify, record → *Consistent operations*

### 2. Developer vs. CEO Mindset
- Developer focus: Does the system work? Correct behavior, performance/integration, testing, and maintainability.
- CEO focus: Can we keep delivering it? Customer value and payment, people and operating costs, and post-launch commitments.

### 3. Users vs. Buyers & Business Reality
- Users (e.g., residents, visitors, guards) prioritize convenience, clear entry instructions, and dependable daily workflows.
- Buyers (e.g., community management) prioritize control, traceability, security, ROI, and contract renewal.
- Post-sale responsibility: Launch is only the beginning—ongoing support, updates, incident recovery, data privacy, and cash discipline determine long-term viability.
- AI & Software Work: AI can draft prototypes, explain errors, and suggest test cases, while humans remain accountable for architecture, integration, validation, security, and customer commitments.

## 💡 Five Lessons to Take Forward

2. Users and buyers may be different
3. Delivery continues after launch
